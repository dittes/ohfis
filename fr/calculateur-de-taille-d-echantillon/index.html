Comment fonctionne Calculateur de taille d'échantillon

Before you run a survey, find out how many answers you need. Enter the confidence level (95% is standard), the margin of error (± 5 points is common) and, if the group is small, the population size. The result is the number of completed responses for a proportion estimate.

The calculation uses Cochran's formula n₀ = z² · p(1 − p) ÷ e², where z is the normal quantile for your confidence level (1.96 for 95%), p the expected proportion (50% is the most cautious, because it needs the largest sample) and e the margin of error. For a finite population N the finite population correction n = n₀ ÷ (1 + (n₀ − 1) ÷ N) shrinks the number: a population of 1,000 needs 278 responses instead of 385. z is computed with an accurate inverse normal function, so any confidence level is supported.

Mode d'emploi

  1. Enter the population size (leave empty if it is very large).

  2. Choose the confidence level and margin of error.

  3. Read the required sample size and plan your survey around it.

Questions fréquentes

Why is 385 so common?

It is the sample size for 95% confidence, ±5% margin and a large population, using the cautious 50% proportion.

Does this account for people who do not respond?

No. Divide the result by your expected response rate to know how many people to invite.
