Cómo funciona Normalizador de audio (subir el volumen)

Drop an audio file (WAV, MP3, M4A, OGG, FLAC or the sound of a video) and raise it to a target level. Peak normalisation makes the loudest point reach the target (−1 dBFS is the usual choice); loudness normalisation matches the average level (about −16 dBFS RMS for podcasts) while keeping the peaks below −1 dBFS. You hear the result before downloading it as WAV.

Your browser decodes the file, the tool measures the peak and the RMS level over all samples, calculates one gain factor (10^(dB/20)) and multiplies every sample by it. Because only a single gain is applied, the dynamics and the noise-to-signal ratio do not change: the background noise gets louder together with the voice. The RMS method is limited so that the loudest sample never exceeds −1 dBFS, which prevents clipping, but it does not compress, so a recording with a few very loud peaks may stay quieter than the target. The output is a 44.1 kHz WAV (16-bit or 32-bit float).

Cómo usarla

  1. Drop the audio file.

  2. Choose peak or loudness mode and the target level.

  3. Listen, then download the WAV.

Preguntas frecuentes

What is the difference between peak and RMS normalisation?

Peak looks at the loudest single sample, RMS at the average energy. Two recordings with the same peak can sound very different in loudness; RMS is closer to what you hear.

Why is my file not as loud as I expected?

A single loud sound (a cough, a click) limits the gain. Cut it out first, or use a compressor, which this tool does not provide.

¿Se suben mis archivos?

No. El archivo lo abre y procesa tu propio navegador. Puedes comprobarlo tú mismo: abre la pestaña de red de tu navegador mientras usas la herramienta, o desconéctate de internet después de cargar la página.

¿Es gratis esta herramienta?

Sí, por completo. No hay registro, ni plan de pago, ni marca de agua en tus archivos.
