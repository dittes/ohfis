How Extract Emails, Phones & URLs from Text works

Choose what you are looking for from a library of tested patterns (email addresses, URLs, phone numbers, UUIDs, IPv4 addresses, hex colours, hashtags, @mentions, dates, numbers and IBANs) and the tool lists every match. If you know regular expressions you can enter your own pattern.

Each pattern is a JavaScript regular expression run over the whole text with the global flag. Results can be de-duplicated, and the counts show how many matches there were in total. Because the patterns are written for real-world text, trailing punctuation such as the full stop after a link is not included in the match. Custom patterns are validated and errors are shown instead of failing silently.

How to use it

  1. Paste your text, logs or HTML source.

  2. Pick what to find, or choose "custom" and type a regular expression.

  3. Copy the list of matches.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know regular expressions?

No. Pick a ready-made pattern from the list. Custom patterns are optional.

Are phone numbers detected in every country?

The pattern recognises common international and national layouts with spaces, dots, dashes or brackets. It finds candidates; it does not verify that a number exists.

Are my files uploaded?

No. The file is opened and processed by your own browser. You can check it yourself: open your browser's network tab while using the tool, or disconnect from the internet after the page has loaded.

Is this tool free?

Yes, completely. There is no sign-up, no paid tier and no watermark on your files.
