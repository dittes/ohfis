How PERT Estimator (Three-Point Estimate) works

Estimates are uncertain, so ask for three numbers per task: optimistic, most likely and pessimistic. The tool computes the expected duration of each task and of the whole project, the standard deviation, the time within which you can be 80% or 95% sure to finish, and the chance of meeting a target date.

For each task the PERT expected value is (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6 and the standard deviation (P − O) ÷ 6. For the project the expected values are added and the variances (sd²) are added, so the total sd is the square root of that sum; the total is treated as normally distributed (central limit theorem), which gives the confidence percentiles and the probability of finishing by a target, from the normal cumulative distribution. Tasks are assumed independent. Dependencies, shared risks or a long critical path make the real spread larger than shown, so use the result as a conversation starter, not a promise.

How to use it

  1. List the tasks with optimistic, most likely and pessimistic values.

  2. Optionally enter a target total.

  3. Read the expected total, the confidence ranges and the probability of hitting the target.

Frequently asked questions

Why not just use the most likely estimate?

Because task durations are skewed: things can take far longer but rarely much shorter. PERT weights the likely value and pulls the expectation toward the pessimistic tail.

What do the 95% figures mean?

If the model is right, there is a 95% chance that the project finishes within that time.
