How Dual Monitor Size & Scaling Matcher works

Enter resolution and diagonal of both monitors. The calculator shows the pixel density of each, the physical size, the height difference and the display scaling to set so that windows and text look equally large on both screens when you drag them across.

Pixel density is the diagonal in pixels divided by the diagonal in inches. If screen 1 has 109 PPI and screen 2 92 PPI, screen 1 needs about 118 % of the scaling of screen 2 to show the same physical text size. Operating systems offer fixed steps (100, 125, 150, 175, 200 %), so the match is approximate; an exact match is rarely possible. Sizes assume square pixels and the diagonal printed on the monitor, which is the visible diagonal.

How to use it

  1. Enter the resolution and diagonal of both monitors.

  2. Read the density ratio and the advice.

  3. Set the scaling in the operating-system display settings.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the diagonal printed?

In the model name or on the box. 27" means 27 inches visible diagonal.

Why not just set both to 100 %?

Then the denser screen shows smaller text and windows jump in size when moved.

Are my files uploaded?
