How Julian Date & Ordinal Date Converter works

Astronomers, satellite operators, manufacturers and old mainframe systems use serial day numbers instead of month and day. Enter a date to get its Julian Day number, Modified Julian Date, ordinal date (2024-061) and day of the year, or enter a Julian Day to get the calendar date and time.

The Julian Day (JD) counts days from noon UTC on 1 January 4713 BC. It is computed from the Unix timestamp as JD = ms ÷ 86,400,000 + 2,440,587.5, which is why midnight UTC always ends in .5. The Modified Julian Date subtracts 2,400,000.5. The ordinal date is the year and the day number within the year (001–366), the form defined in ISO 8601 and used in production and logistics codes. "Julian date" in a food or factory code usually means this ordinal date, not the astronomical Julian Day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Julian date on my product label?

Usually YYDDD or YYYYDDD: the year and the day of the year, such as 24061 for 1 March 2024. It is not the astronomical Julian Day.

Why does a Julian Day end in .5 at midnight?

Julian Days start at noon, so midnight falls half a day later.
