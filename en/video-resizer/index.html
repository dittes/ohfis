How Video Resizer (Change Video Resolution) works

Choose a standard height such as 1080p, 720p or 480p, a width, or an exact width and height, and get a scaled MP4. Smaller videos are faster to upload, take less space and play smoothly on weak connections.

With a standard height or a width, the other side is calculated to keep the proportions; "exact" sets both and can stretch the picture. Making a video larger does not add detail. The video is processed by FFmpeg, the standard open-source video tool, compiled to WebAssembly and running inside your browser. The first use downloads the engine (about 31 MB), which your browser then keeps. Nothing is uploaded. Everything happens in your browser’s memory, so very large videos (several gigabytes) or long 4K clips can fail on a phone or an older computer; try a shorter or smaller clip in that case.

How to use it

  1. Drop the video.

  2. Choose the height, width or exact size.

  3. Press "Resize video" and download.

Frequently asked questions

Which size should I pick?

1080p for general use, 720p for email and chat, 480p or lower for very small files.

Does resizing also shrink the file?

Usually yes, a lot, because fewer pixels need to be stored. Use the Video Compressor to control the file size directly.

Are my files uploaded?

No. The file is opened and processed by your own browser. You can check it yourself: open your browser's network tab while using the tool, or disconnect from the internet after the page has loaded.

Is this tool free?

Yes, completely. There is no sign-up, no paid tier and no watermark on your files.
