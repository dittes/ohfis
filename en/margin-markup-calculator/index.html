How Margin vs Markup Calculator works

Margin and markup both describe profit, but against different bases, and mixing them up is a classic pricing error: a 50% markup gives only a 33% margin. Enter any two of cost, price, margin and markup and see all the others.

Margin = (price − cost) ÷ price: the share of the selling price that is profit. Markup = (price − cost) ÷ cost: the profit as a share of cost. To reach a target margin m the price is cost ÷ (1 − m); to apply a markup k it is cost × (1 + k). Conversions: markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin), margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup). A margin of 100% is impossible, which the calculator reports instead of dividing by zero.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit divided by the selling price; markup is profit divided by the cost. The same sale has a lower margin than markup.

How do I price for a 30% margin?

Divide the cost by 0.70. A cost of 70 gives a price of 100.
