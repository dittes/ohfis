How Braille Translator (Text to Braille) works

Type text and see it in braille cells you can copy, or paste braille characters and read them as text. Capital letters, whole capitalised words and numbers get their indicator signs, and common punctuation is supported. The result consists of Unicode braille patterns (U+2800 block), so it can be used on signs, labels, teaching material and in documents with a braille font.

This is uncontracted (Grade 1) English braille: each letter is one cell from the six-dot patterns (a = dot 1, b = dots 1-2, c = dots 1-4 and so on), a number sign (dots 3-4-5-6) precedes digits, which reuse the letters a–j, a capital sign (dot 6) precedes a capital letter and a doubled capital sign a word in capitals. Accents are removed. Real-world braille is often contracted (Grade 2) with abbreviations and differs by language, music and mathematics have their own codes; this tool does not replace a certified braille transcription and is not suitable for official signage without checking.

Frequently asked questions

Is this Grade 2 braille?

No. It is uncontracted Grade 1, which is readable but longer than the contracted braille most readers use.

Can I print braille with this?

The output is Unicode text. Braille on paper needs an embosser or a braille font and proper formatting.

Are my files uploaded?

No. The file is opened and processed by your own browser. You can check it yourself: open your browser's network tab while using the tool, or disconnect from the internet after the page has loaded.

Is this tool free?

Yes, completely. There is no sign-up, no paid tier and no watermark on your files.
